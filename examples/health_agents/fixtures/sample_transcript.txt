Doctor: Good morning, come in. What brings you here today?
Patient: Morning doctor. I've had a bad cough for about five days now, and since
yesterday I've been running a fever.
Doctor: Okay. Is the cough dry or are you bringing up any phlegm?
Patient: It's mostly dry, but this morning there was a little yellowish phlegm.
Doctor: Any chest pain or difficulty breathing?
Patient: No chest pain. I do feel a bit breathless when I climb the stairs, but
not at rest.
Doctor: Any fever measured at home?
Patient: Yes, last night it was 38.9 degrees.
Doctor: Alright. Do you have any ongoing medical conditions?
Patient: I have type 2 diabetes, diagnosed about six years ago. I take
metformin 500 mg twice a day.
Doctor: Any known drug allergies?
Patient: Yes — penicillin gives me a rash.
Doctor: Do you smoke or drink?
Patient: I quit smoking three years ago. I used to smoke about ten cigarettes a
day for fifteen years. I drink occasionally, maybe once a month.
Doctor: Let me check your vitals. Your temperature is 38.5, pulse 96, blood
pressure 130 over 84, oxygen saturation 97 percent. On examination, your throat
is mildly red and I can hear a few crackles in the right lower lung field. No
wheeze. Heart sounds are normal.
Patient: Is it serious, doctor?
Doctor: It looks like a chest infection — likely a community-acquired pneumonia
given the crackles and fever. I'm going to start you on azithromycin 500 mg once
daily for three days. Since you're allergic to penicillin we'll avoid amoxicillin.
Take paracetamol 650 mg up to three times a day if the fever is troubling you.
Patient: Okay.
Doctor: Please get a chest X-ray and a complete blood count done today. Drink
plenty of fluids and rest. If your breathlessness gets worse, or the fever
doesn't settle in 48 hours, come back immediately. Otherwise, let's review in
five days. Keep monitoring your blood sugar closely while you're unwell.
Patient: Thank you, doctor.
